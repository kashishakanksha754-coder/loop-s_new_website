How AI Voice Interview Software Works

By Aadil Raza Reviewed by Akshay, Tech Lead 14 Nov 2026 6 min read

An AI voice interview platform holds a spoken conversation with a candidate, asks structured questions, listens in real time and scores the answers against a rubric the recruiter defined. The hard part is not the voice. It is turning a free-flowing conversation into a score a recruiter can check and defend. This guide explains how the pieces fit together, based on what we built for HYRIXAI, and what to decide before you build your own.

The pipeline behind one interview

Every spoken turn in the interview goes through the same loop:

  1. Speech to text. The candidate's audio is transcribed as they speak.
  2. Reasoning. A language model reads the transcript so far, the question plan for the role and the scoring rubric, then decides what to say next: ask a follow-up, move to the next question, or close the interview.
  3. Text to speech. The reply is spoken back in a natural-sounding voice.

Around that loop sits an orchestration layer. It handles the phone or web audio, works out when the candidate has finished speaking, and lets them interrupt. In HYRIXAI this live-call layer is handled by Vapi, with OpenAI models on the language side, so our own services can focus on interview logic instead of audio plumbing.

Latency is the thing candidates notice first. If the agent takes more than a second or two to answer, people stop sure whether they were heard. Streaming transcription, short model replies and a voice provider with servers near your candidates all help.

From conversation to scorecard

A transcript is not a score. Three design choices decide whether the scores are usable:

  • Fix the competencies first. Define a handful of competencies per role, such as problem solving or communication, and write down what a weak, acceptable and strong answer looks like. HYRIXAI scores voice interviews against a competency scorecard.
  • Ask structured questions. Keep the core questions the same for every candidate so results can be compared. Follow-ups can vary, the core cannot.
  • Make scores checkable. We recommend tying each score to the part of the transcript that supports it. A recruiter can then verify a score in seconds instead of trusting a number.

What sits around the call

The interview is one stage in a hiring process, and a product that only does the call is hard to adopt. In HYRIXAI the call sits inside a wider workflow:

  • AI resume screening before the call, and a short AI voice pre-screen to filter early.
  • Skill assessments with multiple-choice, text, voice and sandboxed coding questions.
  • Recruiter-curated talent pools with search across candidates and jobs.
  • Offer letters with e-signature, and approval workflows with a review stage.
  • Notifications by in-app message, email, SMS, WhatsApp and push.

Keeping humans in control

Hiring is a regulated area, so the human-in-the-loop design is not optional. The EU AI Act lists AI used for recruitment and candidate evaluation among its high-risk uses. New York City's Local Law 144 requires bias audits for automated employment decision tools. In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 governs how candidate data, including recordings, is processed.

Our design response in HYRIXAI is a human review step before any automated rejection, plus a tamper-evident audit log of decisions. The AI recommends, the recruiter decides, and there is a record of both.

This is a design principle, not legal advice. Check the rules that apply to the countries you hire in with your own counsel.

Decisions to make before you build

  • Build the audio stack or use a voice platform. Owning speech and telephony rarely pays off at the start. Use a platform and put your effort into interview logic and scoring.
  • Languages and accents. Test with real candidate audio from your market, not studio samples.
  • Consent and retention. Tell candidates it is an AI interview, what is recorded and how long it is kept.
  • Fallbacks. Decide what happens when a call drops, when the audio is poor, or when a candidate asks for a person.
  • Cost per interview. Voice platforms usually bill per minute of call on top of model usage. Model this before you price the product.
  • How you will measure quality. Compare AI scores with recruiter judgement on a sample of interviews before you rely on them.

The stack we used for HYRIXAI

For reference, this is what sits under the product: React, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS on the front end; Python (FastAPI) microservices behind an API gateway; PostgreSQL, Redis and Kafka; OpenSearch and S3-compatible object storage; Keycloak for login and role-based access; OpenAI models with Vapi for voice calls; Razorpay for payments; and Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform and GitHub Actions for delivery.

FAQ

What is an AI voice interview?

It is a spoken interview run by an AI agent instead of a person. The agent asks questions, listens to the answers, can ask follow-ups, and produces a transcript and a score against criteria the recruiter set.

Can AI voice interviews replace recruiters?

No. They take over the repetitive first-round screening so recruiters can spend time on the shortlist. Hiring decisions should stay with people, and in several regions the law expects that.

What should I check before using one on real candidates?

Tell candidates it is an AI interview, be clear about what is recorded and for how long it is kept, test the system on real audio from your market, review the scoring for bias, and give candidates a way to reach a human.
